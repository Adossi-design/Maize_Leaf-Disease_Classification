How to use this app

This page explains how to take a photo the app can read, what each of the three diseases looks like on a leaf, and where the app is likely to be wrong.

Taking a photo the app can read

  • Photograph one leaf at a time, holding the phone close enough that the leaf fills the square.
  • Take the photo in daylight, and keep the leaf out of deep shadow and out of direct glare.
  • Hold the phone steady and tap the screen to focus before you take the photo, because a blurry photo is hard to read.
  • Choose a leaf that clearly shows the spots or marks you are worried about.
  • Check several plants in different parts of the field, because one leaf cannot tell you how the whole crop is doing.

What this app can and cannot do

What it knows
The app was taught four things and nothing else: Blight, Common Rust, Gray Leaf Spot and a healthy maize leaf. It cannot recognise other crops, insect damage, or problems caused by drought and poor soil, and it will still pick one of its four answers when it sees something else.
How accurate it is
On 629 photos that it never saw during training, the app gave the right answer 93 times out of 100. Most of the photos it learned from were taken close up and in good light, so expect it to be less reliable on photos taken quickly in a field.
Where it struggles
Blight and Gray Leaf Spot both leave long tan marks on a leaf, and the app confuses them. In testing it called 18 of the 86 Gray Leaf Spot leaves Blight, which is about one in five. When the answer is one of these two, treat it as a strong hint rather than a certainty.
Your photos
The reading happens inside your phone, so your photos are never uploaded and nobody else can see them.
Working offline
After you have opened this page once, the app and the model stay saved on your phone, so you can keep checking leaves in the field even when there is no network.
Keyboard
If you are using a computer, press P to read the leaf, C to clear the photo, and 1, 2 or 3 to move between the three sections.
Before you act
This app is a helper, not a diagnosis. Show the leaf to your local agricultural extension officer before you spend money on any treatment.
